Epidemic Control - EpiC Project

Project Description

The Meeting Targets and Maintaining Epidemic Control (EpiC) Project is a global initiative funded by PEPFAR and USAID, led by FHI 360, that works to achieve and maintain HIV epidemic control and strengthen global health security.

Core Objectives: The project is driven by three global HIV targets (95-95-95):

1. 95% of people living with HIV know their status.
2. 95% of those who know their status are on antiretroviral treatment (ART).
3. 95% of people on ART achieve viral suppression.

Tanzania, EpiC targets the following vulnerable and heavily impacted groups: Key Populations (KPs): People at higher risk for HIV, including female sex workers, men who have sex with men, and transgender individuals. Adolescent Girls and Young Women (AGYW): Focuses on economic empowerment, social behavior change, and biomedical HIV prevention (like PrEP).

Interventions:
1. Differentiated Service Delivery: Expanding HIV self-testing, index testing, and network testing to find undiagnosed cases in the community.
2. Biomedical & Care Support: Providing continuous access to condoms, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), and viral load monitoring.
3. Capacity Building: Partnering with local community-based and faith-based organizations to deliver stigma-free services tailored to local needs.
4. Health Security: Integrating epidemic preparedness by supporting case investigations, contact tracing, and strengthening laboratory safety and diagnostic capacity across the country.

My Role & Approach

As a Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Officer for the EpiC Project at SHDEPHA+, I was responsible for designing and implementing the project’s Monitoring and Evaluation Plan to ensure effective tracking of program performance and results. My role included overseeing data collection processes, supervising Data Officers, conducting supportive supervision visits, and ensuring high standards of data quality through routine Data Quality Assessments (DQAs), data cleaning, verification, and triangulation. I prepared and disseminated program reports, monitored project progress against established indicators, and conducted data analysis to generate evidence for informed decision-making and program improvement. Additionally, I provided mentorship and training to project staff, facilitated the utilization of data for strategic planning, and managed M&E budgets to ensure efficient implementation of monitoring and evaluation activities.

Achievements & Results
  • Established the Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) function in Arusha from the ground up, developing core systems including the M&E plan, SOPs, and data management frameworks that improved data use across program sites.
  • Facilitated data use and learning sessions, presenting findings to internal teams and external partners, enhancing program adaptation and evidence-based planning.
  • 100% data accuracy and consistent in multiple sources due to monthly data triangulation and alignment in (DATIM, DHIS2 and Mer Info)
  • Conducted 3 routine data quality assessments (DQAs) including file reviews, data verification, and triangulation, strengthening data integrity and compliance with program protocols.
  • Developed interactive data dashboards and reporting templates, enabling real-time performance tracking and strengthening evidence-based decision-making for program teams.
  • Produced high-quality monthly, quarterly, and annual reports, ensuring timely submission and improving accountability to internal leadership and external stakeholders.
  • Participated in 2 annual project planning and budgeting process, integrating data insights into strategic decision-making and resource allocation.
